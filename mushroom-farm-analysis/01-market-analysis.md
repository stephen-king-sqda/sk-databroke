# 01 — Market Analysis

## Market size and growth

The mushroom market is one of the fastest-growing produce categories in the US.

- **Global mushroom market:** ~$73B (2025) → ~$80B (2026), forecast CAGR ~9% through the early 2030s.
- **US mushroom market:** ~$11.6B (2025) → projected ~$24B by 2034, CAGR ~8.3%.
- **Specialty/gourmet sub-segment** (oyster, shiitake, lion's mane, maitake, king trumpet, chestnut) is the fastest-growing slice — driven by plant-based eating, functional/medicinal food trends, and a wave of restaurants featuring local fungi.

The button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus) still dominates volume (~90% of US tonnage), but the **economics on commodity buttons do not work at small scale** — the major Pennsylvania houses operate at razor-thin margins on tens of millions of pounds. **All of the small-farm money is in specialty.**

## Demand drivers

1. **Plant-based protein** — meaty texture of oysters, lion's mane, king trumpets is a primary chicken/beef substitute trend.
2. **Functional foods** — lion's mane (cognitive), reishi, turkey tail, cordyceps move at very high price points.
3. **Farm-to-table restaurants** — every mid-sized city now has 10–50 restaurants whose chefs actively prefer a local supplier over a US Foods truck.
4. **Local sourcing** — 4.2/5 importance score from buyers (Cornell). Surveyed growers ranked freshness/quality 4.6/5.
5. **Short shelf life is a moat** — oyster mushrooms last 5–7 days. National distributors cannot compete with same-day local harvest.

## Pricing by sales channel (2026 US averages)

| Channel | $/lb (gourmet blend) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Farmers market (direct retail) | **$12 – $16** | Highest price, highest labor per dollar |
| Restaurant direct (chef account) | **$10 – $14** | Steady weekly revenue; fewer SKUs, larger orders |
| Specialty grocery / co-op | **$8 – $12** | Requires consistency, packaging, food-safety paperwork |
| Wholesale distributor | **$6 – $9** | Lowest margin; reserved for surplus |
| CSA / mushroom subscription box | **$14 – $18 effective** | Pre-paid, predictable, builds brand |

Variety premiums on top of the blend:

| Species | Typical retail | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blue/grey/pearl oyster | $10–$14/lb | Workhorse; easiest to grow |
| Pink/yellow oyster | $14–$18/lb | Visual premium, very short shelf life |
| Shiitake | $14–$18/lb | Slower to fruit; strong demand |
| Lion's mane | $16–$24/lb | High demand, medicinal halo |
| King trumpet | $14–$20/lb | Chef favorite, harder to grow |
| Maitake / chestnut | $16–$22/lb | Niche, lower volume, higher margin |

## Competition at the farmers-market tier

In most US metros there are **0–3** dedicated specialty mushroom vendors at any given farmers market. Compared to vegetable vendors (often 10+), this is a **structurally under-supplied category**. The competitive moat is:

- Variety mix (5+ species on the table beats 1)
- Same-day freshness
- Storytelling (chef relationships, growing photos, recipes)
- Value-add SKUs (dried, powdered, tinctures, grow-kits)

## What this tells us

The macro picture is unambiguously favorable: large market, high growth, fragmented supply at the local level, premium pricing for direct sales, and a structural shelf-life advantage for local growers. **Demand is not the constraint** for a small operator — execution (consistent production + sales channel discipline) is.
