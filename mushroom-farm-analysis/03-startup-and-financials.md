# 03 — Startup Costs, Unit Economics, and 3-Year Financial Model

## Three startup tiers

Pick the tier that matches your capital and risk tolerance. Most first-time operators should start at Tier 1 and graduate to Tier 2 once they hit 60+ lbs/week of consistent sales.

### Tier 1 — Garage / basement, buy-blocks model ($3,000–$8,000)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Fruiting tent / shelving (Martha tent or PVC + 6 mil poly) | $400–$800 |
| Ultrasonic humidifier (6L+) | $150–$300 |
| Inline fan + carbon filter | $200–$400 |
| Lighting (LED grow strips) | $100 |
| Temperature/humidity controller (Inkbird) | $80–$150 |
| Hygrometer, scales, harvest knives, sanitizer | $200 |
| First 100 ready-to-fruit blocks | $1,800–$2,200 |
| Packaging (clamshells, labels, paper bags) | $300 |
| Business license + insurance (first year) | $500–$1,500 |
| Farmers market season fees | $400–$1,200 |
| Working capital buffer | $500–$1,000 |
| **Total** | **$4,600 – $8,000** |

This tier can sustain ~30–50 lbs/week. Realistic year-1 revenue: $25k–$45k.

### Tier 2 — Dedicated 400–600 sq ft fruit room, full vertical integration ($15,000–$30,000)

Adds in-house substrate production. **This is the recommended target architecture** for a "farmers market + local supplier" scale operation.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Climate-controlled space (rent or convert outbuilding) | Sunk / $300–$800/mo |
| HVAC mini-split (12k–18k BTU) | $1,500–$3,000 |
| Industrial humidifier (Ideal Air, etc.) | $700–$1,200 |
| Air filtration / HEPA + carbon filter | $600–$1,000 |
| 4-tier wire shelving x 6 (vertical fruiting racks) | $1,200 |
| **Pressure cooker / autoclave** (All American 41.5 qt or larger) | $700–$2,500 |
| **Laminar flow hood** (DIY ~$400, commercial $1,200–$2,500) | $400–$2,500 |
| Sawdust + bran bulk substrate (initial 6-month supply) | $1,500–$2,500 |
| Grain spawn / liquid culture starter | $300–$500 |
| Filter-patch bags (1,000+ count) | $400–$700 |
| Walk-in cooler or used reach-in for harvested product | $1,500–$3,000 |
| Refrigerated transport (used Coleman + 12V cooler or refer panel) | $300–$1,500 |
| Branding, web, point-of-sale (Square reader, banner, tent) | $800–$1,500 |
| Business license, food-handler, liability insurance | $1,000–$2,000 |
| Working capital (3 months of OpEx) | $5,000–$8,000 |
| **Total** | **$15,000 – $30,000** |

Can sustain ~80–150 lbs/week. Realistic year-2 revenue: $60k–$95k.

### Tier 3 — Container farm / 1,000+ sq ft dedicated facility ($50,000–$150,000)

Retrofitted shipping container or commercial buildout. Out of scope for this analysis; only justified once you've maxed out Tier 2 and have signed wholesale contracts in hand.

## Unit economics (per pound, sawdust-block, self-produced substrate)

| Cost component | Cost per lb |
|---|---|
| Substrate (sawdust + bran) | $0.40 |
| Grain spawn | $0.35 |
| Bags + supplies | $0.20 |
| Electricity (HVAC, humidifier, lights) | $0.40 |
| Packaging | $0.30 |
| Labor allocated (at $18/hr, 8 min/lb) | $2.40 |
| **Total cost per lb** | **~$3.65** |
| **Sell price (blended)** | **$12.00** |
| **Gross margin** | **~$8.35 / lb (≈70%)** |

Industry rule of thumb: production cost lands between **$2.50 and $5.50 per pound** depending on whether you make your own substrate, electricity costs, and contamination loss rate.

## Three-year P&L model (Tier 2, owner-operator + 1 part-time helper)

Assumptions:
- 400 sq ft fruit room, 4-tier vertical racks → ~1,600 sq ft of growing surface.
- Ramps from 40 → 80 → 120 lbs/week.
- Channel mix moves toward restaurant wholesale as production stabilizes.
- 15% contamination loss in Y1, dropping to 7% by Y3.

| | **Year 1** | **Year 2** | **Year 3** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avg lbs/week sold | 40 | 80 | 120 |
| Operating weeks | 48 | 50 | 50 |
| Annual lbs sold | 1,920 | 4,000 | 6,000 |
| Blended price/lb | $12.50 | $12.00 | $11.50 |
| **Gross revenue** | **$24,000** | **$48,000** | **$69,000** |
| Value-add SKUs (dried, kits, powder) | $1,500 | $6,000 | $14,000 |
| **Total revenue** | **$25,500** | **$54,000** | **$83,000** |
| COGS (substrate, spawn, bags, packaging) | $3,800 | $6,800 | $9,000 |
| Utilities (electricity, water, gas) | $2,400 | $3,000 | $3,400 |
| Rent / facility | $4,800 | $6,000 | $6,000 |
| Market fees + delivery fuel | $1,800 | $2,400 | $2,800 |
| Insurance + license + permits | $1,500 | $1,800 | $2,000 |
| Marketing (web, print, samples) | $1,200 | $1,500 | $2,000 |
| Part-time labor | $0 | $7,800 | $14,400 |
| Equipment maintenance + supplies | $800 | $1,200 | $1,600 |
| **Total OpEx (excl. owner labor)** | **$16,300** | **$30,500** | **$41,200** |
| **Net income before owner draw** | **$9,200** | **$23,500** | **$41,800** |
| Owner hours/week | 25 | 35 | 40 |
| Effective owner $/hr | $7.10 | $13.40 | $20.10 |

**Break-even on Tier 2 startup capital:** Month 18–24.

**Net margin trajectory:** 36% → 44% → 50% as scale absorbs fixed costs.

## ROI summary

- **Cash-on-cash return** (Tier 2, $22k invested → $41.8k year-3 net): **~190% cumulative by end of year 3.**
- Industry benchmark: 50–100% annual ROI for well-run small operations; some sources cite up to $6–$10 of profit per $1 of substrate invested on oysters.
- **Sensitivity:** drop selling price by 20% (heavier wholesale mix) and year-3 net drops to ~$24k. Drop yield by 25% (chronic contamination) and you barely cover OpEx. Yield and channel mix are the two levers that matter.

## What kills the math

1. **Selling everything wholesale.** Distributor pricing ($6–$9/lb) cuts gross revenue 30–45%. You **must** anchor at retail/restaurant pricing to make these numbers work.
2. **Spoilage.** Mushrooms lost to over-production or no-shows at market is straight burned cash. Year-1 plan: **never grow more than your confirmed sales channels can absorb.**
3. **Contamination spirals.** A single tray of green Trichoderma mold can colonize your entire fruit room. One bad month can wipe out a quarter's profit.
4. **Underpricing your labor.** The model above pays the owner ~$7/hr in year 1. If you cannot bear that, this is the wrong business until you can afford to absorb year-1 economics.
