# 04 — Operations, Production Cycle, and Staffing

## The production cycle, end-to-end (oyster on sawdust block)

| Stage | Duration | What happens | Critical control point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substrate prep | 1 day | Mix hardwood sawdust + soy hull/bran pellets, hydrate to 60–65%, pack 5-lb bags | Moisture % — squeeze test |
| Sterilization | 2.5 hrs @ 250°F + cool-down overnight | Pressure cook to kill competing microbes | Reach + hold core temp ≥250°F |
| Inoculation | 2–5 min/bag | Add grain spawn in front of laminar flow hood | Sterile technique, no airflow through hands |
| Incubation (spawn run) | 14–21 days | Bag fully colonizes (turns solid white) | 70–75°F, dark, no disturbance |
| Initiation (pinning) | 3–5 days | Cut slits in bag, drop to 60–65°F, raise humidity to 95%, introduce light | Air exchange — high CO₂ stalls pinning |
| Fruiting | 5–7 days | Pins balloon into harvest-ready clusters | Humidity 85–90%, daily misting |
| Harvest | 1 day/flush | Cut at base, cool immediately | Cold chain — pre-cool to 38°F within 1 hr |
| Rest between flushes | 5–10 days | Block re-hydrates, pins again | Spray surface, do not soak |
| Total per block | **35–55 days** for 2–3 flushes | | |

Different species shift the timeline — shiitake blocks take 60–90 days, lion's mane is 30–45 days. But the **cadence** is what matters operationally: you want a continuous pipeline of blocks at different stages so you harvest **every single week** of the year, not in batches.

## The weekly cadence (steady state, ~80 lbs/week)

A continuous-production farm doesn't run in big batches — it runs like a Toyota factory. Every Tuesday you inoculate the bags that fruit four weeks from now. Every Sunday you harvest what was inoculated four weeks ago.

**Sample weekly schedule (owner + 1 part-time helper, Tier 2 scale):**

| Day | Owner tasks | Helper tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Substrate mix + bag (60 bags), 4 hrs | — |
| Tuesday | Sterilize overnight load (passive), inoculate ~50 bags in lab, 3 hrs | — |
| Wednesday | Move colonized blocks → fruit room. Restaurant deliveries (3–5 stops), 4 hrs | Harvest morning flush, pack, 4 hrs |
| Thursday | Fruit-room maintenance, climate check, lab cleanup, 3 hrs | — |
| Friday | Harvest, pack for Saturday market, 4 hrs | Harvest, pack, 3 hrs |
| Saturday | **Farmers market 7am–1pm**, 7 hrs incl. setup/teardown | Market support OR rest |
| Sunday | Light harvest, restock displays, admin/email/orders, 3 hrs | — |
| **Total** | **~28 hrs** | **~7 hrs** |

This is the realistic 28–35 hr/week owner profile for a profitable small farm. **It is not a passive business.** Mushrooms don't take weekends off — humidity drops, pins abort, and a single 12-hour HVAC failure in summer can lose you 50% of an active flush.

## Labor model by stage of growth

| Stage | Lbs/week | Owner hrs/wk | Other staff | Annual labor cost (ex-owner) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 garage (Tier 1) | 30–50 | 20–25 | None | $0 |
| Y2 small fruit room (Tier 2) | 60–100 | 30–40 | 1 PT (10 hrs/wk @ $15) | ~$7,800 |
| Y3 mature small farm | 100–150 | 35–45 | 1 PT + occasional market helper | ~$14,000 |
| Scale-up (Tier 3) | 200+ | 40 + management | 1 FT grower + 1 PT | $50,000+ |

**Hiring sequence to follow as production grows:**
1. **Harvest + pack helper** (10–15 hrs/wk). Cheap to train, frees the owner from the most time-consuming task.
2. **Saturday market helper** (~6 hrs/wk). The owner should be selling/networking with chefs, not running the cash register.
3. **Substrate/lab technician** (20+ hrs/wk). Requires real training. Hire only after you have stable demand >150 lbs/week.
4. **Delivery driver** (5–10 hrs/wk). Outsource last — it's the easiest task to keep doing yourself.

## Climate control — the operational bedrock

Mushrooms are essentially **a humidity and CO₂ business**, with mycelium attached.

- **Spawn run:** 70–75°F, 70% RH, high CO₂ (no air exchange), dark.
- **Fruiting:** 60–70°F (species-dependent), 85–95% RH, **air exchange every 1–2 hrs** to keep CO₂ < 1,000 ppm, 12 hrs of low-spectrum light.
- Use a controller (Inkbird ITC-308, AC Infinity Controller 69, or similar) to automate fan + humidifier cycles. **A grower who tries to manage humidity by hand will fail.**

## Cold chain and shelf life

- Oyster: 5–7 days at 38°F. Lion's mane: 4–6 days. Shiitake: 10–14 days. King trumpet: 14+ days.
- **Pre-cool harvested mushrooms within 1 hour.** A simple reach-in refrigerator works at Tier 1; a small walk-in or used refer panel is the upgrade at Tier 2.
- For market: transport in coolers with ice packs even if it's "just an hour drive." Once mushrooms warm up, they sweat and the buyer notices the texture immediately.

## Record-keeping

Maintain a **batch log** for every substrate run:

- Batch ID, species, date inoculated, spawn source, substrate recipe
- Incubation room and shelf location
- Date moved to fruiting
- Harvest dates + lbs per flush
- Contamination notes (color, smell, % of bags lost)

This isn't bureaucracy — it's how you find out, six months in, that batches inoculated on Thursdays contaminate twice as often, because that's when the neighbor mows the lawn next door and you've been leaving the lab door cracked. Real example pattern.
