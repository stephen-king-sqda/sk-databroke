# 07 — Feasibility Verdict and 12-Month Plan

## Is this feasible? Short answer: **Yes, conditionally — and 2026 is the best window in a generation to start.**

A small specialty mushroom farm at "farmers market + local supplier" scale is **financially viable, structurally favorable, and underserved in most US metros**. It is also one of the most operationally demanding small-food businesses you can run. The viability is not the question — fit with the operator is.

**The macro is unusually aligned in 2026.** The US cattle herd sits at 86.2M head — a 75-year low — with the 2025 calf crop the smallest since 1941. Ground beef is at a record ~$6.70/lb and the USDA forecasts another 10% rise in 2026. Beef cannot biologically rebuild before 2028, giving mushroom producers a **3-year window of structurally elevated relative demand** and a brand-new wholesale channel (the mushroom-beef burger blend) that didn't economically exist before 2024. See [08-beef-tailwind.md](./08-beef-tailwind.md).

## Go / no-go scorecard

Rate yourself honestly. Score each 1–5.

| Factor | Why it matters | Self-score |
|---|---|---|
| **Tolerance for 7-day/week physical work** | Mushrooms don't take weekends off; humidity/harvest schedules are unforgiving | __ |
| **Sterile-technique discipline / attention to detail** | The #1 differentiator between profitable and contaminated farms | __ |
| **Sales / interpersonal comfort** | You will cold-call chefs, work farmers markets, and pitch product weekly | __ |
| **Access to $15k–$30k in capital you can risk** | Year 1 will not pay you well; you must be able to absorb that | __ |
| **Access to ≥400 sq ft of climate-controllable space** | Garage, basement, outbuilding, or affordable rental | __ |
| **A metro within 30 min with active farmers markets + farm-to-table restaurants** | Demand side of the equation | __ |
| **A spouse/partner who's on board with the time commitment** | Quiet but real failure factor | __ |

- **30–35:** Strong green light. Start now.
- **22–29:** Yellow light. Address the lowest-scored areas before committing capital.
- **<22:** Red light. Either reframe (hobby scale, not livelihood) or pick a different food business.

## Recommended 12-month execution plan

### Months 1–2 — Foundation (sunk cost: ~$1,000)

- Read [Cornell Small Farms — Specialty Mushroom Cultivation](https://smallfarms.cornell.edu/projects/mushrooms/) end to end.
- Take an online cultivation course (Fungi Academy, GroCycle, or a local extension workshop).
- Grow **3 personal test bags** of blue oyster and lion's mane in a closet to confirm you actually enjoy this.
- Register an LLC, get an EIN, open a business bank account.
- Apply for business license + liability insurance quote.
- Identify your facility: home garage, basement, outbuilding, or low-cost rental ($300–$600/mo).

### Months 3–4 — Pilot production (sunk cost: ~$4,000)

- Set up Tier 1 garage rig: tent, humidifier, fan, controller, shelves.
- Buy **40 ready-to-fruit blocks** from a reputable supplier (North Spore, Field & Forest, Mycoboutique).
- Run two production cycles. Track yields, contamination rate, harvest weights, photos.
- Sell to friends, family, one or two pop-up events. Don't commit to a market yet.

### Months 5–6 — First market season (revenue: ~$4,000–$8,000)

- Apply for **1 farmers market**. Start with one. Master it before adding a second.
- Build the booth: tent, branded signage, chalkboard, samples, recipe cards.
- Set up Instagram + Google Business Profile + a simple website.
- Begin **chef outreach**: 5–10 cold visits with sample boxes. Target 2 standing accounts by month 6.
- Buy blocks weekly; do not vertically integrate yet.

### Months 7–9 — Stabilize and learn (revenue: ~$10,000–$18,000)

- Hit 40–60 lbs/week consistent production.
- 4–6 restaurant accounts.
- Begin **lab buildout**: pressure cooker, basic flow hood (DIY), bag stocks.
- Test producing your own substrate in parallel with buying. Run them side-by-side for 6–8 weeks until your contamination rate on home-made is <15%.
- Start an **email list** from market sign-ups. Send weekly availability email.

### Months 10–12 — Transition and decision point (revenue: ~$8,000–$14,000)

- Switch fully to self-produced substrate (Tier 2 economics activate).
- Hire **first part-time helper** (10 hrs/wk).
- Launch a small pilot **CSA** (10–20 subscribers, 8-week season) for next year.
- Year-end review: are you on track for $30k–$50k of Year 1 revenue? If yes, plan Tier 2 expansion. If you're at <$15k, audit the bottleneck — usually channels, not production.

**Year-1 cumulative revenue target: $25,000–$45,000. Year-1 cumulative profit target: $4,000–$12,000. Year-1 owner labor: 20–30 hrs/wk averaging out to roughly $5–$10/hr — this is the price of building the business.**

## Recommended sequence to maximize profit, not vanity metrics

The operators who make real money — the ones grossing $80k–$150k from a small fruit room — almost universally followed this sequence:

1. **Start with oysters on bought blocks.** Don't fall in love with rare species in year 1.
2. **Win one farmers market first.** Don't try to do three markets and three restaurants simultaneously.
3. **Add restaurants for steady revenue** once production is reliable. They are the cure for spoilage anxiety.
4. **Vertically integrate substrate production** only after you can sell what you produce.
5. **Add varieties one at a time.** Shiitake → lion's mane → king trumpet → maitake. Master each before adding the next.
6. **Value-add (dried, kits, powder) once you have surplus to absorb.** Not before.
7. **Hire help before you burn out**, not after.

## When to *not* do this

Be honest. This is the wrong business if:

- You want passive income. Mushroom farming is the opposite.
- You don't enjoy the science / lab dimension. Sterile technique is the daily craft.
- You don't enjoy selling. Production is half the job; sales is the other half.
- You live in a market with <100k people and no active farmers markets or farm-to-table restaurants.
- You can't tolerate 12+ months before you reach owner-paid-fairly economics.

If those don't disqualify you, the numbers in [03-startup-and-financials.md](./03-startup-and-financials.md) are achievable.

## Final takeaways

- **Demand is favorable, supply is fragmented, prices are strong.** The market is on your side.
- **Tier 2 (~$15k–$30k, 400–600 sq ft) is the sweet spot.** Tier 1 is a good pilot. Tier 3 is premature for most.
- **Channel discipline is what separates profitable farms from busy hobbies.** Farmers market + 6–10 restaurant accounts + CSA is the proven recipe.
- **Contamination and burnout are the two non-financial killers.** Defend against both with discipline and early hiring.
- **At year 3, a focused owner-operator is realistically earning $35k–$50k of net income from 35 hrs/week of work on a $25k investment** in the base case. With the burger-blend wholesale channel actively pursued, year-3 revenue can reach **$110k–$140k** and net income **$55k–$75k**. That's a strong outcome by small-business standards — comparable to a successful food truck and considerably more capital-efficient.
- **The beef tailwind is the difference between a good business and a great one over the next 3 years.** Operators who explicitly position around "local protein at beef-substitute prices" and chase the burger-blend wholesale channel will compound brand and customer relationships faster than they could in a normal beef market.

The verdict: **build it, but build it in the right order.**
