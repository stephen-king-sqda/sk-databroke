# 02 — Growing Methods, Species, and Yields

Cornell Small Farms classifies commercial specialty mushroom cultivation into **four production methods**. Each has very different capital, skill, and seasonality profiles.

## The four methods

### 1. Outdoor logs (shiitake primary)

- **Substrate:** freshly cut hardwood logs (oak, maple, hickory), 3–8" diameter, ~40" long.
- **Inoculate** with spawn plugs/sawdust, seal with wax, let colonize 6–18 months in shade.
- **Force fruiting** by soaking ("shocking") in cold water weekly May–October.
- **Yield:** ~1 lb fresh mushrooms per log per year, **4–5 lbs total per log over 3–4 productive years**.
- **Cost per log:** ~$4.74 material + labor; gross revenue $40–$80 per log over its life.
- **Pros:** Low capital, low tech, premium "forest-grown" branding, no sterile lab.
- **Cons:** Seasonal (May–Oct), 18-month lag before first dollar, weather-dependent, requires land and shade.

### 2. Outdoor totems / stumps / wood-chip beds (king stropharia, wine cap, oyster)

- Wine caps grown in wood-chip beds; oysters on totems or buried logs.
- **Pros:** Very low cost ($1–$2 of spawn per bed), great for CSA add-on, no climate control.
- **Cons:** Seasonal, low yield density, hard to schedule for restaurants.

### 3. Indoor on supplemented sawdust blocks ("the standard")

- **This is the dominant method for commercial specialty production** and what 95% of the financial models in this folder assume.
- **Substrate:** hardwood sawdust + bran (~20%), hydrated to 60–65%, packed into 5-lb bags with a filter patch.
- **Sterilize** at 250°F / 15 PSI for 2.5 hrs in a pressure cooker or autoclave.
- **Inoculate** in front of a flow hood (HEPA-filtered laminar airflow) with grain spawn.
- **Incubate** at 70–75°F for 2–4 weeks until block is fully colonized (solid white).
- **Fruit** in a high-humidity (85–95% RH) room with 600–1000 ppm CO₂ and 12 hrs of light.
- **Yield:** 1.5–2.5 lbs of fresh mushrooms per 5-lb block over 2–3 flushes (biological efficiency 80–150% for oysters, 75–125% for shiitake on blocks).
- **Pros:** Year-round, predictable, can pack vertically (40–50 lbs/sq ft/year of fruiting floor), works for nearly every gourmet species.
- **Cons:** Needs sterile lab discipline, climate control, electricity, contamination is the #1 killer.

### 4. Indoor Agaricus (button, crimini, portobello) on composted manure

- Phased composting + casing + spawn run — capital and labor profile of a **completely different business**. Margins are tiny, scale required is large. **Skip this** for a small-farm strategy.

## Species selection for a farmers-market scale operation

| Species | Difficulty | Cycle (inoc → harvest) | Yield (BE %) | Retail $/lb | Recommended for Y1? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue/pearl oyster** | Easy | 14–21 days | 100–150% | $10–$14 | **Yes — anchor crop** |
| **Pink/golden oyster** | Easy | 14–21 days | 80–120% | $14–$18 | Yes, small qty for color |
| **Shiitake (block)** | Medium | 60–90 days | 75–125% | $14–$18 | Yes, second SKU |
| **Lion's mane** | Medium | 30–45 days | 60–100% | $16–$24 | Yes, premium SKU |
| **King trumpet** | Hard | 60–90 days | 60–90% | $14–$20 | Year 2 |
| **Chestnut / pioppino** | Hard | 60–90 days | 50–80% | $16–$22 | Year 2 |
| **Maitake** | Very hard | 90–120 days | 40–70% | $18–$24 | Year 3 |

**Recommended starter mix:** 70% oyster (workhorse cash flow), 20% shiitake, 10% lion's mane. Add a fourth premium variety in year 2.

## Yield benchmarks (sawdust block method)

- **Per 5 lb block:** 1.5–2.5 lbs harvested over 2–3 flushes
- **Per sq ft of fruiting floor (no stacking):** ~7 lbs/year (industry avg)
- **Per sq ft with 4-tier vertical racks:** 25–50 lbs/year
- **Per sq meter for shiitake:** 10–15 lbs/year
- **Beginner expectation:** use **14% biological efficiency** for the first 6 months, not the 25%+ that experienced growers hit. This single planning mistake is responsible for most year-1 revenue misses.

## Make-your-own substrate vs buy-ready-to-fruit blocks

The single biggest capital and skill decision a new grower makes.

| Path | Block cost | CapEx | Skill required | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buy ready-to-fruit blocks from a supplier (Mycoboutique, North Spore, Field & Forest) | ~$15–$22/block | Low ($3k–$7k) | Low | Tighter (~30–40% gross) |
| Buy spawn, make your own substrate | ~$4–$6/block all-in | High ($15k–$30k for autoclave, flow hood, lab) | High | Better (~55–70% gross) |

**Recommended first 6 months:** buy ready-to-fruit blocks. Use that time to nail down sales channels and harvest discipline. Once you're consistently moving 60+ lbs/week, **vertically integrate** into substrate prep — that single shift roughly doubles gross margin and is the difference between a $40k/year side business and a $90k/year main income.
